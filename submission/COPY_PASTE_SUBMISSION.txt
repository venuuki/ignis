PROJECT NAME
EmberCal

SUMMARY
EmberCal is an Australia-focused web application that makes NASA MODIS and VIIRS hotspot records more comparable across satellite generations. We aggregate quality-filtered detections into common monthly grid cells and learn separate daytime and nighttime relationships during the sensors’ overlap. The calendar expresses historical MODIS activity on a VIIRS S-NPP-equivalent scale, with VIIRS continuation after supplied MODIS support ends.

Users can select a region, enter coordinates or draw an area, explore fire seasonality, compare the latest complete month with a historical baseline, inspect recent NASA FIRMS detections, and download CSV or PNG outputs.

On frozen 2019–2021 national test predictions, monthly count error against the VIIRS reference falls from 79.4% for raw MODIS to 10.2% after calibration, measured by WAPE. We report remaining uncertainty and regional variation. EmberCal supports historical research and preparedness planning; actual user impact and prospective performance remain to be validated.

PROJECT DETAILS
WHAT WE BUILT

EmberCal is a working Australian prototype for the Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots challenge. It connects a consistent monthly burning-activity calendar with an interactive area selector, recent satellite observations, seasonal comparisons and downloadable results. The installed historical record spans November 2000 to 14 November 2026. Recent observations use a separate NASA FIRMS feed.

THE PROBLEM

MODIS and VIIRS have different pixel sizes, detection sensitivities and observation schedules. A change in raw hotspot counts can therefore reflect the instrument as well as fire activity. A long historical calendar needs a common reference scale before users compare periods across satellite generations.

HOW HARMONIZATION WORKS

We retain source metadata and quality-filtered observations, aggregate detections into common 0.1-degree geographic cells and calendar months, and separate daytime and nighttime counts. During overlap years, we learn the relationship between MODIS and a VIIRS S-NPP reference. The deployed main model uses separate day and night multipliers; a separately fitted LightGBM model handles early Terra-only history before July 2002.

Model fitting, selection and testing have distinct year sets. Training uses 2013, 2016, 2018 and 2023; calibration uses 2017 and 2022; frozen evaluation uses 2019–2021. We save evaluation predictions before refitting the production model. Harmonized history preserves calibrated MODIS while supported, then continues with the VIIRS reference after the final supplied MODIS month. The reference can use scaled NOAA observations when S-NPP is unavailable. We neither add nor average overlapping sensor counts.

WHAT A USER CAN DO

Users start with Australia nationally, then choose a preset, enter coordinates, select a point and radius, or draw an area. They can compare the same calendar month across years, inspect source markers and partial months, identify the usual active window, and compare the latest complete month with a chosen historical baseline. A separate recent-hotspot map offers rolling 24-hour, 72-hour and seven-day views, confidence filters, observation details and CSV download. Historical calendar and summary outputs can be downloaded as CSV or PNG.

For example, the installed national Harmonized record shows approximately 108,010 reference-scale detections for October 2026, compared with a baseline October median of 154,714 using 2003–2022. This is about 69.8% of the median and historical percentile 20. November is incomplete and is excluded from that full-month comparison. Percentile and percentage of median describe different comparisons.

EVIDENCE AND LIMITS

On the frozen 2019–2021 national test, monthly WAPE against the VIIRS reference falls from 79.4% for raw MODIS to 10.2% after calibration. Harmonized WAPE is 13.5%, 11.6% and 21.5% in fixed northern, southeastern and southwestern samples respectively. These are geographic subsets of temporal tests, not spatially unseen regions or independent field validation.

We retain approximate residual ranges and disclose that nominal 80% national coverage is only 72.2% on the original test. We withhold false zero-width ranges, full-month anomaly labels for partial months, and classification labels with fewer than five baseline values. Offline regional candidates that worsened southern results did not replace production. Experimental climate-based outlooks remain secondary; the app withholds unskillful outlooks when insufficient baseline history prevents a fallback.

Hotspots are detections, not unique fires or burned area. Cloud-free observation exposure and missed fires remain unresolved. The live feed does not automatically retrain the historical calendar. Actual target-user sessions and genuinely new prospective evaluation remain future evidence needs.

CREATIVITY AND INTENDED IMPACT

Our contribution combines transparent sensor transfer with a location-specific workflow that preserves source, baseline and uncertainty information through visualization and export. We favour validated comparability over unnecessary model complexity. The intended audience is Australian land managers, preparedness analysts and fire researchers who need historical context. EmberCal supports research and planning; it is not an evacuation, dispatch or fire-spread system.

TOOLS

We use Python and FastAPI for the API; Polars, DuckDB, NumPy, SciPy and Shapely for data and geometry; and LightGBM for the early-period calibration and experimental outlooks. The frontend uses React, TypeScript, MapLibre GL and Terra Draw, with OpenStreetMap and Natural Earth mapping. NASA FIRMS supplies the fire observations. NASA POWER and NOAA climate indices support the separate experimental climate/outlook components. The statistical fitting runs locally, without an external model inference API.

RESOURCE CONTEXT
NASA FIRMS is the fire-observation source. NASA POWER and NOAA indices support separate experimental climate/outlook components, not the main count-calibration multipliers. The NTRS calibration paper concerns radiometric/reflectance continuity; it does not validate our hotspot-count multipliers. The FRP paper is background only; EmberCal does not claim to harmonize FRP or burned area. Screenshots are team application captures with map attribution. Charts use project evaluation outputs.
